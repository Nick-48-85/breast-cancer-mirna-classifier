01 — Dataset

TCGA Breast Cancer Cohort

miRNA expression profiles were sourced from the National Cancer Institute's Genomic Data Commons (GDC) portal. Four binary classification tasks were constructed: Normal vs. each of Stages I–IV. The severe class imbalance — especially Stage IV (15 samples) — drove the choice of stratified cross-validation and guided metric interpretation.

Stage IV's high accuracy (84.6%) must be interpreted cautiously — it is based on only 15 cancer samples and evaluated via 5-fold CV rather than a held-out test set.

02 — Methodology

Multi-Family Consensus Pipeline

Rather than relying on a single feature selection paradigm, the pipeline runs 12 methods across three fundamentally different algorithmic families and aggregates their outputs into consensus miRNA panels using frequency-based voting.

03 — Results

Classification Performance

Each stage presents a different challenge. Stage II benefits from the largest sample pool (595 cases) but sacrifices specificity. Stage III is the hardest — low sensitivity (8%) reflects its molecular ambiguity. Stage IV's apparent strength is inflated by extreme class imbalance.

Dashed line = 40% minimum sensitivity threshold set by the team. Stage III fails to meet it, suggesting miRNA expression alone is insufficient for late-intermediate staging.

Traditional methods (Chi-Square, LASSO) deliver the most consistent accuracy. Deep Learning produced many empty consensus panels at lower k values.

AUC distributions across all stages and feature selection families. LR and SVM-linear show the tightest spread — more predictable across configurations.

Each point is one (stage, family, k, consensus, classifier) configuration. Ideal configurations cluster in the upper-right quadrant. Note how Stage III (red) clusters near high specificity / low sensitivity — models learn to predict "no cancer" almost exclusively.

04 — Biomarkers

Consensus miRNA Biomarkers

Across all stages, the Traditional family's consensus panels (using majority voting) consistently returned a small set of well-studied oncomiRs. The chart below shows which miRNAs appeared in multiple stages — cross-stage presence suggests broad relevance to breast cancer biology.

Notable Biomarkers

  • hsa-mir-203a — selected in all 4 stages; known tumor suppressor in multiple cancers
  • hsa-mir-143 — 3 of 4 stages; frequently downregulated in breast cancer
  • hsa-mir-148a — 3 of 4 stages; linked to metastasis suppression
  • hsa-mir-21 — classic oncomiR; overexpressed in most breast tumors
  • hsa-let-7b — 3 of 4 stages; let-7 family is a key regulator of oncogenes
Consensus Method Features selected by at least 2 of the 4 Traditional methods (Chi-Square, LASSO, SFS, Variance Threshold) per stage. The majority-voting rule trades recall for stability — only features agreed upon by multiple independent methods are retained.
05 — Conclusions

Key Takeaways

📉

Traditional methods win

Chi-Square and LASSO produced the most reliable consensus panels. Deep Learning feature extractors generated empty panels at smaller k, limiting their utility in low-sample regimes.

⚖️

Class imbalance dominates

Stage IV (15 samples) shows inflated accuracy because classifiers learn to predict "normal" almost exclusively. Cross-validation mitigates but cannot fully correct for a 1:17 class ratio.

🧬

Stage III is an open problem

8% sensitivity at 94% specificity means the model rarely detects Stage III cancer. miRNA expression alone appears insufficient — integration with CNV or methylation data is the recommended next step.

📋

Consensus adds robustness

The multi-family approach produces biomarker panels validated by independent algorithmic perspectives. Recurring miRNAs like mir-203a and mir-143 align with established breast cancer literature.
